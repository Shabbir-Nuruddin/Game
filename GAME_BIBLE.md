# TRUST ISSUES — Game Mega-Prompt / Bible

*Use this as the single source of truth when briefing anyone (an artist, a
writer, an AI tool, a publisher) on what this game is. It reflects what's
actually built, not an original pitch — the game pivoted once during
development (see History at the bottom).*

---

## 1. One-line pitch

**A gothic vampire-castle rage-platformer where every trap is a betrayal, and
dying is the entertainment.**

## 2. Elevator pitch (30 seconds)

You're the Heir to a vampire bloodline, descending your own family's castle
one blood-soaked floor at a time. Every obvious path is a lie: the inviting
platform collapses, the safe-looking spikes erupt late, the shiniest door is
death. You WILL die — a lot — and it's funny, not frustrating, because every
death teaches you exactly one thing and the game never blames you twice for
the same trick. Built for one-more-try compulsion loops, shareable death
counts, and the specific joy of watching someone else confidently jump into
an anvil.

## 3. Genre & comparables

**Troll / rage platformer**, the same shelf as *Level Devil*, *Trap
Adventure*, *Getting Over It*-adjacent "unfair game" comedy — but reskinned
gothic-vampire instead of the genre's usual candy-kingdom or meme aesthetic.
Fast sessions, instant respawin, no real stakes beyond a death counter and
bragging rights.

## 4. Theme & tone

**Black-and-blood gothic vampire castle.** Night-only. Palette: near-black
stone, candle-gold accents, blood-red highlights, a huge blood moon.
Everything is dripping, cracked, or on fire in a lantern. The tone is
*melodramatic horror played for laughs* — the game is constantly, cheerfully
lying to you, and its narration (death lines, room warnings, menu flavour
text) treats your death as inevitable and a little bit funny rather than a
punishment. Think Castlevania's silhouette and Diablo's UI language, crossed
with a troll-game sense of humor.

Working title: **Trust Issues**. The name is literal — the whole design
premise is "don't trust anything in this castle," and it doubles as the
tone: the game itself has trust issues with the player and vice versa.

## 5. The player character

**"The Heir"** — a vampire noble (default skin, cape and all) walking down
through their own ancestral castle. Not a hero; nobody sent them here.
Nine other unlockable skins exist (Crimson Lord, The Spectre, Golden Cursed,
Shadowbound, Pink Menace, Ashen Slayer, plus three Crypt-Shop-only skins:
Bone Pale, Nosferatu, Royal Blood) — cosmetic only, never pay-to-win, though
several carry a small signature mobility trick (a dash, a double-jump, a
speed boost) tied to progression achievements rather than purchase.

## 6. Core mechanics

- **Movement:** coyote time, jump buffering, snappy gravity, squash-and-stretch —
  standard tight platformer feel, tuned so death always feels like YOUR
  mistake, never the controls'.
- **The central lie:** every level teaches "the obvious path is the trap."
  After one death to a given trap, you're expected to recognize and dodge it
  next time — the game is teaching a vocabulary of betrayals, not grinding
  you down.
- **Death is cheap and funny:** instant respawn, screen shake, a comedic
  death line ("Impaled.", "Crushed by the falling stone.", etc.), a running
  death counter that's the game's actual scoreboard.
- **Room rules, not just traps:** beyond individual hazards, whole ROOMS can
  break one promise at a time — the candles go out and you can only see a
  small circle around you (Dark), the coffin/exit runs from you until it's
  cornered (Flee), the ceiling descends and you must keep moving (Press),
  a curse reverses your controls while you're inside (Reverse), or walking
  back out a doorway loops you to the start until you find the way to break
  it (Loop). Exactly one rule per room, then it retires — nothing is allowed
  to become a pattern you can autopilot.
- **The castle learns you back ("reactive traps"):** floor spots where you
  lingered safely in a past attempt get quietly wired with a NEW trap on
  retry — the castle is reacting to how you played it, not just replaying
  the same script. (Difficulty-gated; off entirely on Casual.)
- **Gravity flips:** later floors introduce gravity runes — cross one and
  you fall UP to the ceiling, mirroring the level's hazards.

## 7. The Bestiary (trap roster)

Every trap you've died to is catalogued in the in-game **Vampire's
Bestiary** — a persistent codex, unlocked one page at a time by dying to
something new for the first time. 19 entries:

Iron Spike (always lethal, always visible — "the game being honest for
once"), Ambush Spike, Breathing Spike (grows/shrinks on a rhythm), Spike
Rain, Treacher-Floor (the collapsing platform), Bait Crusher, Thwomp Stone,
The Chandelier (a wide hanging Thwomp), Stake Launcher, Whirling Blade,
Pendulum Blade, Flame Jet, Holy Water (a puddle that turns lethal on a
pulse), Swooping Bat, Cursed Spring, Hex of Confusion (reverses controls),
Banishment Rune (yanks you back to the start), False Coffin (the obvious
exit is the fake one), Sunbeam Trap (an invisible hazard on safe-looking
ground).

## 8. Progression & modes

- **The Castle** — the main campaign: 40 hand-designed floors across four
  10-floor "worlds" (Castle → Crypt → Swamp → Throne), each with its own
  colour mood. Every 10th floor is a boss or a 6-room "exam" testing
  everything that world taught.
- **Bosses:** the Ghoul (grounded stun-charge brawler), the Countess
  (teleporting decoys), the Warlock (shielded ranged caster with fog), and
  the Vampire Lord — a shapeshifter who wears the first three bosses'
  forms as disguises before dropping into his true one.
- **Blood Moon** — a nightly 5-floor gauntlet with a shared daily seed:
  everyone plays the SAME layout tonight, and "Tonight's Rumor" hides one
  secret rule (e.g. "the saws lie tonight" — every saw is harmless) that
  the game only whispers cryptically; proving it is the shareable moment.
- **Endless Night** — infinite procedurally-escalating floors, checkpointed,
  for a pure high-score chase.
- **Multiplayer (Versus)** — live head-to-head races down the same
  procedural track via Photon, with sabotage buttons to troll your rival
  mid-race and a running match score.
- **Difficulty:** Casual / Normal / Nightmare, one global setting that
  scales lives, boss telegraph time and speed, whether the reactive-trap
  system is active, and whether a creeping "sunrise" pressure wall exists —
  the explicit design goal is that a wide audience can FINISH the game on
  Casual without diluting the brutal Nightmare core veterans want.

## 9. The meta-game (between-run systems)

- **Blood Shards** — the death currency: you earn them by dying (yes,
  dying pays), spent in the **Crypt Shop** on cosmetics ONLY (skins, death
  effects, movement trails, gravestone taunts) — never power.
- **Charms** — one equippable perk unlocked by BOTH floor progress and
  shard price (e.g. Gravedigger's Cut doubles death-shard payout, Grave Ward
  no-sells your first death on a floor once).
- **The castle remembers you:** a local "haunting" layer — the menu greets
  you differently after time away and can call out a literal rage-quit from
  last session; your worst trap ("nemesis," 5+ lifetime kills) wears a
  small gold crown in-level and taunts you; other real players' actual death
  locations appear as tombstones on a floor ("Heir-412 died here"), whispering
  their cause of death when you approach.
- **Haunt a Friend (Curse)** — after a run, send a link that plants YOUR
  red ghost on the exact floor you died on, taunting whoever opens it; they
  break the curse by clearing that floor with fewer deaths than you, which
  hands them a fresh counter-brag link. The whole viral loop is one MIT
  Curse link, no server required.
- **Leaderboards** (daily / endless / castle) and a native share card
  ("I cleared tonight's Blood Moon with N deaths — beat that").

## 10. Visual direction

Menus and UI are hand-painted, ornate gothic-fantasy artwork (black stone,
gold hairline borders, blood-red diamonds, dripping display type, a huge
red moon) — think a Diablo-style inventory/menu skin. In-game levels use
that same illustration set for every trap (the Bestiary art IS the
in-level trap art, not a separate style), a near-black mountain-valley
backdrop lit almost entirely by the blood moon's red glow, and stone
platforms with a lit top edge and dripping blood down their face. No
bright colour anywhere outside the moon and the gold UI accents — the
"cute pastel platformer" look was explicitly abandoned (see History).

## 11. Audio direction

Per-theme music tracks (one mood per world/mode), Kevin MacLeod CC-BY
library cues (attribution is a hard legal requirement, always shown in
Settings), plus SFX for jump/death/click/tap and a spoken death groan
channel with its own volume slider.

## 12. Platforms & controls

Cross-platform: desktop/keyboard (WebGL for itch.io-style distribution)
and native Android (APK, sideloadable, IL2CPP/ARM64). On touch, movement is
a joystick that appears wherever your thumb lands (no fixed on-screen
clutter) or arrow-glyph buttons, per player preference; touch Settings
covers stick behaviour, pad size and left/right-handed mirroring instead of
desktop key-rebinding, which doesn't apply to a phone.

## 13. What makes it distinct from its genre peers

1. A trap vocabulary you're explicitly TAUGHT (the Bestiary) rather than
   memorized through blind repetition — the game wants you to feel smart,
   not just persistent.
2. Room-level lies, not just object-level traps — the whole space can
   betray a promise, once, then retire it.
3. A meta-layer of asynchronous haunting (other real players' deaths,
   send-a-curse) that most single-player troll platformers don't attempt.
4. A difficulty system built to let casual players actually finish it
   without gutting the "brutal" identity the genre is chasing.
5. A fully-committed gothic vampire art identity instead of the genre's
   usual candy/meme skin (see History — this was a deliberate mid-project
   pivot, not the default).

## History (for context, not to repeat to press)

The project began as a candy-kingdom platformer starring a yellow blob
named "Beanie" (the README and itch.io copy still describe this version).
It was fully repositioned to the gothic vampire-castle identity described
above — new title, new mascot ("the Heir"), new palette, new UI language —
and everything from Section 4 onward describes the CURRENT, shipping
identity. Any brief, asset, or copy referencing "Beanie," a candy kingdom,
or a pastel palette is describing the abandoned direction, not this game.
